\chapter{Results}

\section{Current Result Scope}

The current result set answers two research questions through four completed experiments, alongside a set of historical exploratory records. RQ1 uses two complementary interventions: seven AI-assisted, researcher-directed and researcher-reviewed controlled near-neighbour suites, each with 50 clusters and field-specific prompt variants; and a source-grounded public-document intervention that removes cited information from exact original documents. RQ2a is a matched-content fixed-candidate mechanism study. RQ2b is a source-grounded, strict-gold full-library study over 2,433 skills and 381 prompts (243 controlled and 138 public-gold). Historical representation and retrieval matrices based on frozen benchmark version \texttt{benchmark-v0.4-2026-06-16} are retained separately for provenance and portability context.

Section \ref{sec:rq1-results-field-isolation} reports the completed RQ1 controlled and public-artifact evidence. Section \ref{sec:rq2a-confirmatory-results} reports the completed matched-content RQ2a confirmatory study over 280 untouched clusters, 600 prompts, 22 conditions, and 13,200 aligned decisions. Section \ref{sec:rq2b-full-library-results} reports the completed full-library RQ2b validation, including first-stage candidate retrieval, fixed-Top-20 reranking, failure decomposition, and recorded cost. Later frozen-v0.4, M6, and external SkillRouter sections are retained as exploratory, diagnostic, feasibility, or portability evidence; they must not be read as the final RQ2a or RQ2b answer. Graph/tree retrieval and downstream execution remain outside the active scope.

\section{RQ1 Results: Controlled and Public-Artifact Evidence}
\label{sec:rq1-results-field-isolation}

\subsection{Public-Skill Primary-Document Prevalence}

Before testing whether a field changes routing, we checked whether the proposed information types actually recur in public skill artifacts. A frozen source frame of 29,292 canonical public primary skill documents was reviewed in 31 batches by a Codex \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra} agent using \texttt{xhigh} reasoning. The agent used a simple binary rule: a field received 1 when it was expressed anywhere in the complete primary document and 0 otherwise. The review included YAML frontmatter, including \texttt{name} and \texttt{description}, and every Markdown section, table, and example; it did not inspect attached scripts, references, or other auxiliary files. Thus the census measures information present in the agent-visible primary skill document, not only information stated in the Markdown body.

The review is model-assisted rather than independent human annotation and was completed in 31 recorded batches, so it is used as descriptive context rather than as a public annotation dataset or quality certification.

\subsection{Controlled Field-Isolation Results}

The clearest current RQ1 results are the completed field-isolation suites for use condition, input/precondition, output/artifact, dependency/resource, boundary/not-for, success/verification, and workflow/procedure. These results are reported before the broader retrieval matrix because they answer the thesis-first question directly: whether one operational field helps separate semantically similar skills when non-target information is controlled.

Each suite contains 50 near-neighbour clusters. Most suites use one direct and one paraphrased prompt per cluster; dependency/resource replaces the paraphrase with a contextual variant, and boundary/not-for adds an implicit-authority variant. Within each cluster, the three sibling skills share all non-target fields. The hidden condition exposes only their shared context, so they are intentionally tied. The exposed condition presents the same context plus one candidate-specific field value. Top-1 and MRR use tie-aware scoring. BM25 and Qwen \texttt{text-embedding-v4} are the primary retrievers; DeepSeek and Codex selectors are secondary reasoning checks. The suites were created with AI assistance under a researcher-defined rubric and mechanically checked. The researcher then reviewed and approved all 350 rows with the labels visible; this was not an independent blinded review.

For both retrievers, Top-1 improves after the target field is shown in all seven suites. The suites are separate authored test sets, however, and are not used to rank fields against one another. Input/precondition remains robust under paraphrase, reaching 91.0\% BM25 and 94.0\% Qwen Top-1. Output/artifact shows a clear retriever difference: its paraphrased rows reach 53.3\% with BM25 and 92.0\% with Qwen. Use condition is also less robust under paraphrase than under direct trigger wording. Dependency/resource is highly effective when it names a concrete capability such as a required tool, platform, credential, runtime, or version; this claim does not extend to generic installation commands.

The more conditional fields remain informative but harder to match. Boundary/not-for falls to 62.0\% BM25 and 58.0\% Qwen under implicit-authority prompts. Paraphrased success/verification rows reach 48.0\% for both retrievers, and workflow/procedure reaches 56.0\% BM25 and 50.0\% Qwen under paraphrase. These values show sensitivity to how the request expresses scope, acceptance gates, and procedure order. Full prompt-subset Top-1 and MRR tables are retained in the experiment artifacts; Table~\ref{tab:rq1a-cluster-bootstrap} provides the main controlled summary.

The target-field values were not forced to equal length. In particular, gold boundary and dependency values are often longer or more detailed than their alternatives. This does not undo the paired result that exposing candidate-specific information changes routing, but the controlled effect should be read as the value of the field content as written, not as a length-normalised estimate of field meaning alone.

The dependency/resource result is strong, but its claim boundary is important. In this suite, dependency/resource means external capability compatibility: required platforms, tools, APIs, runtimes, permissions, versions, or resources. When the field is hidden, all siblings are tied at 33.3\%. When exposed, BM25 reaches 100.0\% top-1 across direct and contextual prompts, and Qwen embedding reaches 99.0\% combined top-1. The added version-specific clusters make the suite harder: version rows are solved by BM25 and by Qwen contextual retrieval, but Qwen direct version rows fall to 88.9\% because one React 18 prompt ranks a React 17 sibling slightly higher. This is useful evidence that version compatibility can be semantically subtle, and that project context such as \texttt{package.json}, \texttt{.nvmrc}, or dependency metadata can clarify the route.

This result should not be generalised to raw dependency/setup text. In the public source artifacts used to build the public-document intervention, concrete constraints such as Chrome DevTools MCP, Firebase CLI, GitHub authentication, Node or React versions, and package manifests commonly coexist with lower-value setup text such as \texttt{npm install}, \texttt{pip install}, or \texttt{npm ci}. The distinction is functional: an installation command normally describes preparation after a skill has been chosen, whereas ``Requires the Chrome DevTools MCP server'', \texttt{GH\_TOKEN}, or ``Node.js 20+'' can rule skills in or out before selection because they describe a required external capability. The thesis-facing claim is therefore conditional: capability compatibility is a strong routing signal when sufficient capability context is visible to the selector. That context can be stated in the user request, but it may also need to be supplied by the routing system from project files, configured tools, available credentials, package metadata, or runtime state. DeepSeek and Codex subagents both reached 100.0\% exposed-field accuracy for dependency/resource, which supports this narrower capability-compatibility framing.

We also measured the score gap between the gold skill and the highest-scoring alternative. This gap increased in all fourteen field--retriever rows, and every 95\% cluster-bootstrap interval was above zero. In other words, showing the field usually moved the gold skill farther ahead of its strongest competitor; the Top-1 result was not produced only by a deterministic tie break. This does not identify an objectively closest alternative: both alternatives were authored as plausible siblings, but the units do not claim that one is semantically closer than the other.

These are supporting checks of whether the distinctions are understandable, not the primary retrieval result.

An additional negative-control suite tests \texttt{examples/tests}. It is not counted as an eighth operational field. In that suite, all operational fields are shared by sibling skills and only the examples/tests section differs. Generic prompts have no true operational gold because all siblings can perform the task; they test whether examples create arbitrary attraction. Rare exact-match prompts deliberately resemble one stored example and estimate the upper bound of example attraction. The corrected multi-method result supports treating examples/tests as workflow-support context rather than a primary routing signal. On generic prompts, exposing examples does not improve retrieval: BM25 moves from 33.3\% hidden to 22.7\%, TF-IDF remains approximately tied at 33.0\%, and Qwen embedding moves from 33.3\% to 28.0\%. DeepSeek and Codex subagent selectors both mark generic rows as ambiguous when examples are visible. On rare exact-match prompts, all methods reach 100.0\% selection of the example-matching candidate. This shows that examples can strongly steer routing when the request copies or closely resembles a stored example, but that effect is better described as example-attraction or possible misguidance than as proof of a distinct skill capability.

\subsection{Public Original-Document Removal}

The second RQ1 experiment applies the same question to preserved public skill documents. A source-tracked pool of 1,099 documents supplies 82 candidate groups. Before scoring, the sources, near-neighbour groups, cited field lines, prompts, and gold skills were checked. For each group and field, the selector sees either the intact documents or the same documents after every cited line carrying that field has been removed from all candidates. The implementation blanks those line contents and leaves the remaining text unchanged. The candidates, prompt, source hashes, and gold skill remain fixed. An edited skill may be incomplete or non-executable because this experiment tests routing after information removal, not execution after editing. The researcher approved all 194 scored gold-label cases and all 402 scored removals with the labels and document edits visible; this was not an independent blinded review. Appendix~\ref{app:rq1-intervention-examples} gives concrete examples of both RQ1 interventions; those examples were selected without using their retrieval outcomes.

This audit explains why the external result should be used as portability evidence rather than as a direct replacement for the local controlled benchmark. \subsection{M6-v2 Semantic Field-Matching Calibration}

The first frozen-v0.4 implementation used a conservative score blend of 0.70 first-stage score and 0.30 semantic-field score. A later calibration sweep varied this blend and the penalty scale using existing candidate-level outputs. The sweep should be read as a diagnostic calibration result, not as a final tuned method, because full-set best weights can overfit the benchmark.

\section{Historical Exploratory Interpretation}

The frozen-v0.4 results support six exploratory observations that motivate, but do not answer, the new RQ2a:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Representation matters under controlled confusability.} With Qwen held fixed, R2 structured representation improves over R1 flat representation on controlled prompts, and nearly matches full skill text under Qwen reranking.
  \item \textbf{Skill-specific retrieval raises the ceiling but preserves the representation question.} SkillRouter substantially improves over generic Qwen on controlled prompts, but R2 and full artifact representations still behave differently under the same SkillRouter architecture.
  \item \textbf{Full skill text is not automatically best.} On public-gold, full-skill Qwen embedding performs much worse than R1 or R2. More raw text can dilute the retrieval signal.
  \item \textbf{Lexical candidate generation is a serious baseline.} M6-v1 with TF-IDF/schema or TF-IDF/flat first stages performs strongly on frozen v0.4. The thesis should not frame the comparison as only embedding versus structure.
  \item \textbf{Public-gold is a harder external-validity check.} Public-gold results show that descriptions and provider/task cues remain powerful, while schema extraction and lexical field matching are brittle for messy public skills.
  \item \textbf{The current field-aware method is diagnostic, not final.} M6-v1-local supports the value of field-aware matching, but a stronger semantic or LLM-assisted field matcher is needed before claiming a robust structure-aware retrieval method.
\end{enumerate}

Overall, the current evidence supports an information-centred thesis, but the strongest claim should be phrased carefully: preserving operational selection information helps under controlled semantic confusion, but the way that information is extracted, represented, and matched determines whether the benefit transfers to public-authored skills.
